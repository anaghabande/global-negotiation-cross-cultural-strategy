# Global Negotiation & Cross Cultural Strategy

## Overview

This repository presents selected graduate level work focused on global negotiation, cross cultural management, cultural intelligence, and international business strategy.

The projects apply management frameworks to business situations involving the United States, Japan, and Malaysia, examining how culture influences communication, trust, decision making, conflict, negotiation strategy, and international business relationships.

## Featured Projects

### U.S. Japan Trade Agreement: Global Negotiation Analysis

A strategic analysis of the U.S. Japan Trade Agreement examining the negotiation from economic, cultural, and managerial perspectives.

Key areas of analysis include:

• BATNA and negotiation leverage  
• Stakeholder interests and power dynamics  
• Integrative and distributive negotiation strategies  
• Value creation and value claiming  
• Cross cultural negotiation  
• Trust and relationship building  
• International trade strategy  
• Cultural adaptation  

### U.S. Malaysia Cross Cultural Case Analysis

An analysis of communication and management challenges between U.S. and Malaysian teams in a global technology project.

The analysis applies:

• GLOBE cultural dimensions  
• Cultural Intelligence  
• Power distance  
• Institutional and in group collectivism  
• Direct and indirect communication  
• Cross cultural trust  
• Global team management  

### Japanese Business Culture

An examination of Japanese culture and its implications for global managers, with particular emphasis on:

• Power distance  
• Uncertainty avoidance  
• In group collectivism  
• Organizational loyalty  
• Consensus based decision making  
• Hierarchy and workplace relationships  

### Trust & Communication in Japanese Culture

An analysis of how Japanese cultural norms influence professional communication and relationship development.

Key themes include:

• Relationship based trust  
• High context communication  
• Nonverbal communication  
• Indirect disagreement  
• Workplace hierarchy  
• Group harmony  
• Cross cultural communication

## Managerial Perspective

The projects demonstrate an important principle of global management: effective strategies cannot always be transferred unchanged across markets and cultures.

Global managers must understand stakeholder interests, cultural expectations, communication patterns, trust mechanisms, and negotiation dynamics before determining how to lead, communicate, and make decisions across borders.
